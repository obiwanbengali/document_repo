Evidence briefing

From the NHS Pathways estate to a clinically governed Bayesian architecture

Four connected investigations, each answering a different architectural question.

The work has moved beyond a simple monolithic-versus-federated comparison. We first measured the Pathways estate, then looked for candidate family boundaries, then tested the computational and operational implications of the architecture choices. The ENT investigation now takes that evidence into a concrete first-family definition for the proposed April 2027 private beta.

The investigations are deliberately separated because they use different evidence and answer different questions. Structural evidence can suggest a family boundary, but it cannot clinically approve one. Likewise, benchmark or cost evidence can compare implementation patterns without deciding which clinical pathways belong together.

Investigation map

Where the architecture work now sits

The four reports form one evidence chain, with ENT acting as the first concrete family-boundary investigation.

Estate Structure

Understand the NHS Pathways estate

“What does the estate actually look like?”

Measures pathway size, reuse, connectivity, handovers and the classifications already present in Pathways. This establishes the structural constraints any Bayesian representation has to accommodate.
Complete — view evidence Candidate Family Discovery

Find plausible model boundaries

“If we use family BNs, where might the boundaries lie?”

Compares existing Pathways classifications, graph structure and shared clinical features to identify provisional family groupings, overlap, boundary pathways and shared evidence.
Complete — candidates require clinical review Architecture Feasibility & Comparison

Test whether the candidate architectures are practical

“How large, fast, deployable and costly would the options be?”

Covers representation sizing, CPT growth, inference benchmarking, runtime deployment patterns, infrastructure and cost, and decision-evidence synthesis.
Evidence assembled — decision remains governed ENT Family Definition

Define the first clinically reviewable family BN

“What belongs in Ear, Nose and Throat, and where are its boundaries?”

Lists every proposed ENT pathway and the reason for inclusion, then traces internal, incoming and outbound pathway transfers to expose family boundaries, safety escalations and cross-family evidence for clinical review.
Active family investigation — private-beta candidate Revised ENT Evaluation

Test the revised family against observed journeys

“How much 111 Online activity can the revised family cover end to end?”

Uses the explicitly agreed Revised ENT pathway list to measure journey coverage, end-to-end completion, family leakage, out-of-scope handovers and every structural endpoint reachable from the supported family boundary.
Revised scope — journey evidence report
Current architectural direction under investigation

A centralised inference service containing multiple clinically governed family Bayesian Networks, potentially supported by a deliberately small shared/common evidence layer. Federation describes the model topology and governance boundary; it does not imply one microservice per family.

Architecture feasibility stages

The six technical investigations behind the architecture comparison

These replace the shorthand 3A–3F labels in reader-facing material.

Representation sizing

How much model structure must be represented?

Variables, states and family-size baselines.

CPT growth

How quickly does probability data grow?

Parent-count and state-cardinality sensitivity.

Inference benchmarking

What drives runtime feasibility?

Measured latency under controlled topology and cardinality scenarios.

Runtime architecture

How are family models hosted and coordinated?

Central service patterns, loading, routing, state and failure modes.

Infrastructure & cost

What operational footprint do the patterns imply?

Capacity, HA floors, workload assumptions and comparative cost.

Decision synthesis

What evidence belongs in the ADR?

Trade-offs, open gates, caveats and decision evidence without algorithmically choosing a winner.
